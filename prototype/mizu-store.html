Our story

Founded by athletes who were tired of throwing cups away

Mizu started with a simple frustration: a surf trip, a thermos that tasted of last week, and a bin full of paper cups. We rebuilt the vessel around one rule — the drink only ever touches steel, ceramic or glass.

Today the same principle runs through every tumbler, mug and flask we make, and it is why our products are backed for life rather than for a season. Fewer, better vessels. That is the whole idea.

Lids, gaskets and straws go on the top rack. Wash the vessel itself by hand with warm water and a bottle brush — dishwasher detergent is abrasive enough to dull a powder-coated finish over a few hundred cycles, and the vacuum seal lasts longer without the heat cycle.

A fired ceramic layer inside the steel gives coffee a completely neutral surface — no metallic edge on a light roast, and coffee oils rinse off instead of building up. It is the finish we recommend if you drink filter coffee or anything fermented, and the one to avoid if you routinely drop your flask on concrete.

Filled to the shoulder with 93 °C water and closed, a 16 oz tumbler is above 60 °C at the twelve-hour mark and iced drinks hold below 8 °C for a full day. Two things halve those numbers: filling it half full, and pre-chilling nothing — rinse with boiling water first and you buy back about an hour.
